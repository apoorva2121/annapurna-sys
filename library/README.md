# The starter recipe library

Standard Indian restaurant recipes with typical quantities, so onboarding a new restaurant is
**match, don't create**: *"Your menu has Butter Chicken — here's a standard recipe; your portion looks
larger than standard, adjust the chicken from 250 g to 300 g?"* The chef corrects rather than
composes. `docs/onboarding-cold-start.md` calls recipe entry the thing that "kills more restaurant
software deployments than any bug ever will" and sets a **two-hour** target for a 120-item menu.
This library is what makes that target reachable.

**Status:** content, built 2026-09-12. Consumer is M04 · Stock Items & Recipes. Not yet loaded by
any code; `library/validate.py` runs in `make check` so it cannot rot silently before M04 arrives.

---

## What it is not

**It is not `seed/`.** `seed/` is *Spice Garden* — one synthetic restaurant with prices, popularity
weights, planted anomalies and a golden food-cost figure (~26%) that `seed/validate.py` asserts.
Putting 200 dishes there would move that figure, break the golden tests and bloat the demo to an
unrealistic 230-item menu. The library is the **catalogue a restaurant's menu is matched against**;
`seed/` is one restaurant's selection from it. Same YAML shape, so M04 needs one loader; different
purpose, so different files.

**It carries no prices and no popularity.** Both are per-restaurant. Prices bootstrap from a city
market-rate table and are corrected on first purchase (`onboarding-cold-start.md`); popularity is
observed demand. A library that shipped either would be shipping a guess dressed as a default.

**It is not verified by anyone with a ladle.** See *Confidence*, which is the most important section
in this file.

## Layout

```
library/
  README.md            this file
  ingredients.yaml     the ingredient master — superset of seed/ingredients.yaml, same codes
  preparations.yaml    bases and sub-recipes — superset of seed/preparations.yaml, same codes
  dishes.yaml          dishes and their variants (Half / Full / Piece)
  validate.py          structural + calibration rules; runs in `make check`
```

Codes are shared with `seed/` on purpose. A dish in the seed and the same dish in the library are the
**same recipe**, so a restaurant onboarded from the library and the demo restaurant agree — and
`validate.py` asserts that the overlap is quantity-for-quantity identical. If they ever drift, the
calibration claim below is false and the build says so.

## Confidence — the rule this library lives by

Every dish and every preparation carries a `confidence` tier. **No entry may claim more certainty
than it has.** A recipe that says "estimated, confirm with your chef" is worth more than one that
states 250 g of chicken as fact, because the first one gets corrected and the second one gets
believed.

| Tier | Meaning | How it earned the label |
|---|---|---|
| `seed_calibrated` | Present in `seed/`, passes the golden food-cost tests against a ~26% target | Copied programmatically from `seed/`, never retyped. `validate.py` asserts byte-identical quantities |
| `derived` | Built from a calibrated template by substitution — same structure, same ratios, different protein or vegetable | The template is named in `basis` and is itself `seed_calibrated` or `derived`. Substituting into an `estimated` recipe yields another `estimated` recipe; the validator refuses the promotion |
| `estimated` | Domain knowledge, no calibration anchor. May still name a `basis` as a hint to the reader | Nothing. A chef must confirm it, and the onboarding flow must say so |

`validate.py` refuses a dish without a tier. `PROVENANCE.md` (generated by the validator) states the
fraction in each tier — and the honest number for a first release is that **most of the library is
`estimated`**. That is the correct state for content a chef corrects; the tiers exist so nobody
mistakes it for content a chef verified.

Ingredient metadata (`yield_factor`, `shelf_life_days`, `storage`) for rows not in `seed/` is
likewise estimated. It is stated once here rather than per row, because every non-seed row is in
the same position.

## Portion conventions

Stated once and applied everywhere. All derived from the seed's eleven calibrated dishes, so a
library recipe sits next to a seed recipe without a visible discontinuity. Quantities are per plate
in base units (`g` / `ml` / `unit`).

| Plate | Convention |
|---|---|
| **Main course gravy, FULL** | 230–270 g gravy base · protein: paneer 170–180 g, chicken curry-cut 290–300 g, boneless 220 g, mutton 250 g, prawn 180 g · 15–25 g finishing fat (butter/ghee/cream) |
| **Main course gravy, HALF** | ≈ 62% of FULL across every line. Offered on the dishes restaurants actually sell by the half |
| **Dry / semi-dry veg main** | 200–260 g primary vegetable · 20–30 ml oil · masala lines |
| **Dal / legume main, FULL** | 200–330 g base preparation · 20 g finishing fat |
| **Tandoor starter, FULL** | 200 g paneer · 300 g marinated chicken · 190 g mushroom · 65–70 g marinade where not pre-marinated |
| **Fried starter, FULL** | 210–240 g protein or 200–250 g vegetable · 45–60 g batter flours · 70–100 ml oil |
| **Biryani / pulao, FULL** | 300–320 g cooked rice · 260 g protein or 180 g vegetables · 35–40 g birista · 20–25 g ghee |
| **Plain rice** | 300 g cooked (`PREP_RICE_BOILED`) |
| **Bread, PIECE** | 110 g naan dough · 100 g atta for lachha/paratha · 60 g atta for roti · 90 g dough for kulcha |
| **Indo-Chinese, FULL** | 220–240 g protein · 160 g noodles or 300 g cooked rice · 45–60 g cornflour where battered · 45–90 ml oil |
| **South Indian** | 120 g dosa batter per dosa · 3 idlis ≈ 150 g batter · 200 ml sambar per serve · 40 g chutney |
| **Soup, FULL** | 250 ml |
| **Dessert, PORTION** | 100–150 g |

Oil on fried items is **absorbed plus carry-over**, following the seed (Chicken 65 at 90 ml,
Prawn Koliwada at 100 ml). It is deliberately higher than a home recipe would state, because a
restaurant fryer is not a home pan and the cost is real.

## Preparation conventions

- A preparation is a **base made in a batch that feeds several dishes** — a gravy, a marinade, a
  batter, a dough, a chutney, a stock. A thing made once per plate is a recipe line, not a prep.
- `batch_yield` is one realistic morning batch for a 40–60 cover restaurant. `shelf_life_hours` is
  **hours**: a gravy made from seven-day cream dies in thirty-six.
- Nesting is at most **three** levels (ingredient → prep → prep → dish, ADR 0006). The seed's
  `ONION → PREP_BIRISTA → PREP_YELLOW → dish` chain is the depth the validator enforces.
- Yield is the *usable output*, not the input mass. Birista yields 1200 g from 4000 g onion, and that
  loss is the point.

## Categories

The seed's six, plus what an Indian menu actually has:

`SOUP` · `STARTER_VEG` · `STARTER_NONVEG` · `MAIN_VEG` · `MAIN_NONVEG` · `RICE_BREAD` · `CHINESE` ·
`SOUTH_INDIAN` · `ACCOMPANIMENT` · `DESSERT`

Beverages are deliberately absent — they are not recipe-driven in the same way, and the bar is out of
month-1 scope.

## Extending it

- **Add a dish**: copy the nearest entry, change what differs, set `confidence: derived` and name
  the template in `basis`. Setting `seed_calibrated` by hand is a validation error — that tier is
  earned only by being in `seed/`.
- **Add an ingredient**: it needs `base_unit`, `yield_factor`, `shelf_life_days`, `storage`,
  `category`. No price.
- **Add a preparation**: it must be made by the batch. Usually that shows as two or more consumers;
  the validator warns on a single consumer so the author confirms it is not a recipe line in
  disguise, and `single_consumer_ok: true` with a `notes` line silences it deliberately.
- **Never** edit a `seed_calibrated` entry here. Change it in `seed/` and re-copy, or the golden
  tests and the library disagree about the same dish.

Run `python library/validate.py` — it is also part of `make check`.
