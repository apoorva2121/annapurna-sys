# Competitor Business Numbers — Restaurant Tech (India-focused)

**Scope:** Business numbers only — customers/outlets claimed, revenue/profit by financial year,
funding, valuation, headcount, founders/HQ, acquisitions, and derived metrics (ARPU, revenue/employee,
growth %). This file does NOT re-cover product features or app-store reviews — see
`docs/product/competitor-research/01-platforms.md`, `02-play-store-apps.md`, `03-point-solutions.md`
for those; pricing headlines are pulled from those files by reference where already captured, not
re-fetched.

**Date of research:** 2026-09-13.

**Method:** Public-source web research (WebSearch + WebFetch) against business press, MCA/RoC-filing
aggregators (Entrackr, Inc42, Tofler, Tracxn, The Kredible, YourStory, VCCircle, Moneycontrol,
Economic Times), company press releases, LinkedIn/Tracxn estimate pages, and (for Toast) SEC filings.
No filing was pulled directly from MCA; all filing-derived figures are as reported by the
named outlet, one layer removed from the primary document.

**Confidence labels (mandatory on every figure):**
- **[filed]** — from a statutory filing (MCA/RoC, SEC), as reported by a named outlet.
- **[reported]** — business press coverage without a filing cited.
- **[vendor]** — the company's own claim (website, press release, founder interview).
- **[estimate]** — third-party estimate (Tracxn, Growjo, Owler, LinkedIn headcount, etc).
- **[conflict]** — sources disagree; all values listed, not averaged.

**Caveat:** Public sources only. Indian private-company filings are frequently 12–18 months stale
by the time they are reported (FY25 filings, e.g., often surface mid-2026). Some paywalled sources
(Entrackr, The Morning Context) could not be fetched in full; where that happened it is noted and
the finding is based on search-result snippets only. Numbers copied between blogs are traced to the
earliest source found where possible, but this is not exhaustive. Nothing here should be treated as
current as of the reader's date — always check the cited article's own date.

  "customers/brands" claim, not a paying-account count matched to the FY24/25 filed-revenue period.
  A naive ₹39.9 Cr / 25,000 ≈ ₹16,000/customer/year is given only illustratively.

### Notes on gaps

  available.
- No FY-by-FY loss figure found (only revenue); Restroworks may be closer to breakeven than
  Petpooja given the flat FY25 revenue and no news of large recent loss-making rounds, but this is
  inference, not a sourced number.

## Market share picture

Every statement below is an **[inference]** drawn from the numbers above, not a sourced market-share
figure — no source found in this research directly states India restaurant-tech market share for
any company.

- **Petpooja looks like the volume/installed-base leader among India-specific restaurant POS
  vendors** — its 100,000+ claimed clients dwarf every other restaurant-specific competitor
  (Restroworks 25,000+, LimeTray 4,500+, TMBill 14,000+, Recaho 15,000+), and it is also the
  best-covered by business press (three funding rounds, four FY's of revenue, a recent 3.5x
  valuation step-up). **[inference]:** this combination of scale, funding momentum, and coverage
  suggests Petpooja is the most-referenced name in Indian restaurant POS distribution.
- **Restroworks/Posist looks like the enterprise/chain-account leader**, not the volume leader — a
  much smaller headcount (123) and revenue (₹40 Cr) than Petpooja but named enterprise clients
  (Taco Bell, Subway, Nando's) that Petpooja's SMB-volume motion doesn't compete for. **[inference]:**
  the two companies may not actually compete head-to-head for the same restaurant segment as often
  as their both appearing in "top India restaurant POS" lists suggests.
- **DotPe/Rista and UrbanPiper are the aggregator-integration layer**, not POS vendors first —
  DotPe's Series C investor list (PayU, a payments company) and UrbanPiper's investors (Swiggy and
  Zomato themselves) suggest **[inference]** these companies are as much strategic infrastructure
  for the aggregator platforms as independent restaurant-tech vendors, which may explain why
  UrbanPiper can sustain heavy losses (₹50 Cr in FY25) without visible existential pressure — its
  investors have a platform interest beyond pure financial return.
- **On revenue, DotPe (₹82.8 Cr) and Petpooja (₹102.3 Cr FY25 / ₹77 Cr FY24) are the largest India
  entities found in this file** among the pure restaurant/merchant-tech names — both larger than
  Restroworks (₹40 Cr) and UrbanPiper (₹53 Cr) despite Restroworks and UrbanPiper having
  longer operating histories or larger claimed footprints. **[inference]:** claimed customer count
  and filed revenue do not move together across this set — a company's marketing "customers served"
  number is a poor proxy for its financial scale.
- **Vyapar and myBillBook (FloBiz) dominate on raw user count (10-15M and 5M+ respectively) but are
  not restaurant-specific** — they are general MSME billing/accounting apps that happen to be used
  by some restaurants. **[inference]:** if this repo's competitive set is meant to capture
  "restaurant owners' financial software choices" rather than "restaurant POS vendors" specifically,
  these two plausibly reach more individual restaurant owners than any dedicated restaurant-POS
  vendor in this file, simply by being general-purpose and free/cheap enough for a small dhaba to
  adopt for GST billing alone — but this is speculative, since neither discloses a restaurant-
  specific customer count.
- **A large tail of small, likely-bootstrapped India restaurant-POS vendors (group 12: Restrofi,
  PosBytz, Recaho, DineOpen, TMBill, Torqus, Zobaze) exists below the venture-backed names**, none
  with any financial-press footprint. **[inference]:** this is consistent with restaurant POS being
  a relatively low-capital-intensity, easily-replicable SaaS category in India — many small vendors
  can survive on direct sales/channel partnerships without needing (or attracting) VC capital, which
  matters for Restaurant OS's own competitive assessment: **the ceiling for a well-funded platform
  play (Petpooja/DotPe-scale) coexists with a floor that many unfunded small vendors can profitably
  occupy** — the middle (LimeTray, SalaryBox-scale, ₹9-10 Cr revenue, shrinking) looks like the
  least attractive position to be in among everything researched here.
- **Toast is roughly two orders of magnitude larger than any India company in this file** ($6.15B
  vs. Petpooja's ₹102 Cr ≈ $12M) — **[inference]:** even India's best-funded, fastest-growing
  restaurant-tech company (by the numbers here) is still an early-stage business by the standard of
  the US market's maturity, which is consistent with this repo's own vision-doc framing of the
  Indian restaurant-tech market as still forming rather than consolidated.

## Numbers that conflict or look inflated

1. **Petpooja's 100,000+ claimed clients vs. its FY24/FY25 filed revenue and its own published
   pricing floor.** `01-platforms.md` (line 57, already in this repo) cites Petpooja's own
   India-market pricing at roughly ₹3,000-12,000/month depending on tier (~₹36,000-1,44,000/year).
   Even at the *lowest* disclosed price point (~₹10,000/year, per a separate estimate cited in this
   file's Petpooja section), 100,000 paying clients would imply **at least ₹100 Cr/year in revenue
   from pricing alone** — yet FY25 filed revenue is ₹102.3 Cr *total*, which would leave nothing for
   any client paying above the rock-bottom tier, no revenue from Petpooja's other product lines
   (Purchase/inventory, POS hardware, etc. per `01-platforms.md`), and no attrition/inactive-account
   discount at all. **This strongly suggests the 100,000+ figure is a lifetime/cumulative-install
   claim, not an active-paying-customer count** — worth treating with real skepticism if used for
   competitive-sizing purposes.
2. **DotPe FY25: ₹79.1 Cr profit stated on the same page as ₹137.9 Cr in expenses against ₹82.8 Cr
   revenue.** Flagged in detail in DotPe's section above. Either the profit figure, the expense
   figure, or both are wrong in the source (Inc42's financial tracker), or there is a large
   unexplained other-income line not surfaced by the fetch. Not resolved — presented as unresolved
   conflict, not corrected.
3. **UrbanPiper's own tracker page shows two different FY25 revenue figures on the same page**
   (₹53.1 Cr vs. ₹29.9 Cr) with two different growth directions (-6.5% vs. +17.57%). Not resolved.
4. **LimeTray FY24: positive PAT (+₹59.8 Lakh) stated alongside a -11.6% net margin** — arithmetically
   incompatible; the positive-PAT reading is more consistent with the other disclosed figures
   (expenses ₹7.6 Cr against ₹9.3 Cr revenue) and is treated as the more likely correct one, but
   both are reported since neither was independently confirmed against the underlying filing.
5. **MarketMan's CBInsights-listed "$1.5M total funding, $6M valuation (2016)"** looks
   implausible against MarketMan's own claim of "15,000+ restaurant operators" and "$50B+ in
   annual client transactions." A platform processing $50B in claimed annual transaction volume
   almost certainly required far more than $1.5M in total invested capital to build; the CBInsights
   profile is marked "Unclaimed" (unverified by the company), which is the likely explanation —
   the profile is stale/incomplete rather than the true number.
6. **Restroworks/Posist: Tracxn's funding page ("$508K over 3 rounds") is internally inconsistent
   even on its own terms** — the three amounts it itemizes ($206K + $528K + $666K) sum to ~$1.4M,
   not $508K — and, separately, is almost certainly missing all funding after 2015 given the
   company's subsequent scale (25,000+ customers, enterprise chain clients across 50+ countries).
   Treat Restroworks' "total funding" as unknown, not as $508K or $1.4M.
7. **Getlatka's ARR estimates run roughly 4x above filed revenue for both Restroworks (~$18-20.5M
   Getlatka vs. ~$4.8-4.9M filed) and are not checkable for the others** — Getlatka's methodology
   (self-reported ARR interviews, per its own site framing found elsewhere) appears systematically
   more generous than MCA-filed revenue for at least this one case; any Getlatka figure in this
   space should be discounted accordingly rather than taken at face value.
8. **Zomato Hyperpure's reported 30-35% YoY segment revenue decline** sits oddly alongside Eternal
   management's own framing of Hyperpure as a growth-supporting "sourcing backbone" for Blinkit's
   expansion — the most likely explanation offered by the press coverage itself (a reclassification
   of revenue into Blinkit following its shift to an inventory-led model) is plausible but was not
   independently confirmed against Eternal's segment-reporting notes in this pass.
